Phase 1 Research Findings

Read this first. It changes the conclusions of the documents that follow.

The architecture in this pack is sound and unchanged. What changed is what the model predicts.

We built the prediction engine on a laptop before provisioning any AWS infrastructure, at zero cost, and tested it honestly. Predicting direction - will the price go up - produced no usable edge. Predicting volatility - how much the price will move - produced a real, statistically significant one. The platform therefore forecasts the size of the move, not its direction.

What we measured

The volatility result is significant at p = 0.002 on a Wilcoxon signed-rank test across ten independent, non-overlapping test windows. It beat persistence - the naive forecast that next week looks like this week, and a genuinely strong benchmark in volatility work - on every single window.

Why direction failed and volatility worked

This was measurable before any model was trained:

Normalised ATR (14-day)0.370 0.044
Realised volatility (20-day)0.281 0.028

Roughly thirty times more signal in the volatility column. Volatility clusters - calm weeks follow calm weeks, turbulent weeks follow turbulent weeks. That persistence is one of the most robust empirical facts in finance. Direction, on the fifty most heavily traded stocks in India, is close to a fair coin, because thousands of professionals with faster data have already traded those patterns away.

How we know the result is real

A positive finding has to survive tests that a null finding passes trivially. We ran all of them.

ControlRank correlation What it proves
Real labels0.276the result
Labels randomly shuffled 0.020 signal destroyed - this is not leakage

Deliberate leak (answer as an input) 1.000 near-perfect, as it must be - the gate catches this

The shuffled-label control is the important one. We scrambled the answers at random and retrained; the model scored 0.020, which is no skill at all. Had it retained skill, information would have been reaching the model through some path other than the intended features, and the result would be an artefact.

The deliberate leak is run on purpose so the safety threshold is demonstrated to fire rather than assumed to.

How accurate is it

There is no single "percent correct" figure, because the output is a range rather than a yes-or-no call. Three measures matter:

MeasureResultMeaning
Interval honesty89.5% We promise a 90% range; the price stayed inside it 89.5% of the time. When we say 90% we mean 90%.
Rank correlation0.315 When the model says a week will be turbulent, it usually is. The naive forecast scores 0.234.
Error reduction8.5% Lower RMSE than persistence, on all ten test windows, p = 0.002.

Interval honesty is the one that matters most for trust. An overconfident model is dangerous, because position sizes are computed from these numbers and the error compounds in the over-sizing direction.

How this compares to the state of the art

We checked the published research rather than assuming. Two 2026 papers are directly relevant.

On return forecasting, a study testing the pretrained foundation models - TimeGPT, Chronos, TimesFM, Moirai - against trained baselines concluded that gains over a random walk are "small and sparse", and that these models are "useful practical priors" but not "universal engines of statistically reliable alpha generation". The same paper notes that out-of-sample R-squared in the published cross-sectional literature is "typically below 1%". Ours was -0.5%, which is inside the normal range.

On volatility forecasting, a study of nine foundation models found that only one beat the standard Log-HAR econometric benchmark at every horizon, "and by a narrow margin" - a ratio of 0.982 against 1.000, so a 1.8% improvement. Ours is 8.5% over persistence. Different benchmark and different universe, so not a direct comparison, but it establishes the scale: the state of the art is contesting a few percent on this problem, not tens of percent.

The practical conclusion from that literature, and from our own result, is the same: choosing the right problem matters more than choosing a fancier model.

This is a stronger commercial position than the alternative. Any technically literate prospect who sees 98% directional accuracy in a proposal concludes the authors do not understand financial machine learning, and discounts the whole document. A proposal that reports a modest number beside its baseline, and shows a correct walk-forward harness with its controls, demonstrates competence that cannot be faked.

What was built to verify this

ControlWhat it guarantees
Walk-forward validation, 10 folds Always trained on the past, tested on the future. k-fold cross-validation is prohibited on time series.
Purge, 5 sessions A training row whose 5-day answer window reaches into the test period is removed.
Embargo, 5 sessions A gap after each boundary, because the day after training ends is nearly a duplicate of the last training day.
46 automated tests Including nine that deliberately introduce each bug to prove the leakage tests can actually fail.
Fold-fitted scaling Normalisation fitted per fold on training data only. Fitting across the whole dataset leaks future highs and lows backwards.
Block bootstrap, 21-session blocks Confidence intervals that respect the autocorrelation created by overlapping 5-day windows. A naive bootstrap gives intervals 6.2 times too narrow, which turns noise into apparent significance.
Deterministic feature build Rebuilding produces a byte-identical file, so any number can be reproduced and explained.

Data problems found and fixed

Each of these was invisible - nothing errored, and the data looked reasonable on inspection.

ProblemConsequence if missed
Three wrong stock identifiers. Kotak Mahindra Bank and Bajaj Finance had stale ISINs; Tata Motors no longer exists under that name. A stale identifier returns no data and no error. We would have trained on 47 stocks while believing we had 50.
A phantom 40% crash. Tata Motors demerged on 14 October 2025. Shareholders received a share in the new commercial- vehicle entity, so the true economic return was -0.65%, but the price series shows -40.15%. The largest single move in the dataset was an event that never happened. It would have dominated training and corrupted that stock's 200-day averages for 200 subsequent sessions.
Split adjustment unverified. The data provider does not document whether prices are adjusted for splits and bonuses. We tested it against four known corporate actions rather than assuming. A 1:5 split left unadjusted reads as a -80% return. They are adjusted; demergers are not.

Known limitations

Stated rather than omitted. Each, if left unsaid, would make the result look better than it is.

  • Survivorship bias. The universe is today's NIFTY 50 applied to all history, so the sample is companies selected partly because they succeeded. This inflates results. Correcting it needs curated NSE reconstitution circulars.
  • Dividends excluded. Price-return adjustment only.
  • One feature not built. Delivery percentage, which is genuinely informative in Indian markets, needs a separate NSE bhavcopy ingestion path.
  • No LSTM. The sequential encoder in the design was not trained. The source paper for that architecture found the LSTM lost to XGBoost alone, so this is a deliberate deferral rather than an omission.
  • No strategy simulation. Round-trip cost on NIFTY 50 delivery trades is roughly 22 to 30 basis points, which consumes about a tenth of a typical one-sigma weekly move. We make no profitability claim.

Recommended next step

Build the AWS pipeline as designed in the documents that follow, with the volatility model in place of the direction model. The data layer, feature pipeline, training workflow, promotion gate and dashboard are all unchanged - only the target and the on-screen labelling differ.

AWS System Architecture

How an Indian stock price-forecasting platform is built on AWS — which service does what, why each one was chosen, how data flows from the stock exchange to the screen, and what it costs.

Version 3.0 · Date 29 October 2026 · Region ap-south-1 (Mumbai) · Status Proposed — for approval · Data provider Upstox
How to read this document

No AWS knowledge is assumed. Every service is explained in plain words before it is used, with a short note on why that service and not another. If you only have five minutes, read Section 1 and Section 9.

What this system does and does not do

It does: collect stock market data, learn patterns from history, and show forecasts on a private dashboard — an estimated closing price, an expected high and low, and a probability of gain.

It does not: place orders, touch money, or give investment advice. There is no broker connection for trading. This is a research and forecasting tool.

1The system at a glance

Five stages, left to right. Data comes in, gets cleaned, a model makes a forecast, the forecast is checked for risk, and a person sees it on a web page.

In plain words

Think of it as a factory with five rooms. The first room receives raw material (stock prices, news, oil and currency numbers). The second is a warehouse where every delivery is kept, labelled with its date. The third is a workshop that cleans the material and turns it into measurements a machine can read. The fourth is where four specialists each answer one question about the future. The fifth is the shop window where a person sees the result.

2Every AWS service, explained

AWS sells about 240 services. This system uses 18. Each card says what the service is, what it does here, and why it was picked over the alternative.

2.1 Getting data in

Amazon EventBridge SchedulerScheduling

An alarm clock for the cloud.

Starts every scheduled job at the right Indian time — fetch candles at 16:00, run the forecast at 08:30, retrain on Sunday night.

Why this: it understands time zones natively, so schedules are written as "08:30 Asia/Kolkata" instead of doing UTC arithmetic by hand and getting it wrong twice a year.

AWS LambdaCompute

Runs a small piece of code on demand, then disappears.

Fetches historical candles, downloads news, serves dashboard API requests. Roughly 12 separate functions.

Why this: you pay per millisecond of execution. These jobs run for seconds a day, so a permanently running server would be idle 99.9% of the time.

Amazon ECS FargateCompute

Runs a container for as long as you need, without managing a server.

Holds the live WebSocket connection to Upstox for the whole trading session, decodes the binary Protobuf messages, and also runs model training.

Why not Lambda: Lambda stops after 15 minutes. The market is open for 6 hours 15 minutes. Lambda physically cannot hold this connection — this is a hard limit, not a preference.

Amazon S3Storage

Unlimited file storage. Cheap, durable, never full.

The data lake. Every raw download, every cleaned dataset, every trained model and every evaluation report, each stamped with its date.

Why this: about ₹2 per GB per month, and it keeps 11 nines of durability. It is the memory of the system — if everything else is deleted, S3 can rebuild it.

2.2 Preparing the data

AWS GlueETL

A managed data-cleaning engine (Apache Spark underneath).

Adjusts prices for stock splits and bonuses, computes all technical indicators, joins news and macro features onto the right dates, and runs the data-quality checks.

Why this: rolling calculations over five years of data for 50 stocks is genuinely a Spark-sized job. Glue gives Spark without a cluster to run.

Amazon AthenaQuery

Run SQL directly on files in S3. No database to load first.

Investigating data problems, checking for gaps, and ad-hoc research questions like "show me every day RELIANCE moved more than 5%".

Why this: pay per query (about ₹400 per terabyte scanned). The alternative, a data warehouse, charges by the hour whether you query it or not.

2.3 Making the forecast

SageMaker Training JobML

A managed machine that starts, runs a training script, saves the result and shuts down.

Trains the four XGBoost models every Sunday at 02:00. About 5 minutes, roughly $0.50 a run.

Why this and not a plain container: it gives model versioning, lineage and a registry without extra code — and you pay only for the minutes it runs. Section 4.3 covers the choice in full.

SageMaker Batch TransformML

Loads saved models, scores a batch of rows, writes the answers, shuts down.

Runs the daily forecast at 08:30 — 50 rows in, 200 numbers out, about 3 minutes.

Why not a real-time endpoint: an endpoint bills 24 hours a day for a job that runs once. Batch Transform bills only the 3 minutes — about $3/month for both jobs together, against ~$145 for an idle endpoint.

Risk & Opportunity EngineOur code

Not an AWS service — plain Python running in Lambda.

Checks the four forecasts agree with each other, measures how uncertain they are, and can return No Trade when the answer is not trustworthy.

Why separate: keeping it out of the models means risk rules can change without retraining anything.

2.4 Storing and showing results

Amazon Aurora PostgreSQLDatabase

A managed PostgreSQL database that AWS keeps running and backed up.

Stores every forecast, what actually happened afterwards, model versions, user accounts and saved watchlists.

Why a real database: the dashboard asks relational questions — "accuracy for banking stocks last quarter, by model version". That is SQL's job. Use Serverless v2 so it scales down when nobody is looking.

Amazon API GatewayAPI

The front door for the dashboard's requests.

Publishes about 6 REST routes, checks the login token on each one, and limits how fast anyone can call them.

Why this: authentication, rate limiting and request validation come built in. Writing those by hand is a week of work and a security risk.

Amazon CognitoIdentity

Manages who can log in.

Email and password plus an authenticator app code. Only invited users exist — there is no public sign-up.

Why this: storing passwords safely is easy to get wrong. Cognito handles hashing, resets, sessions and MFA as a managed service.

Amazon CloudFront + S3Hosting

A global delivery network in front of static files.

Serves the React dashboard with HTTPS from a location near the user.

Why this: the dashboard is a folder of files. Putting it on a web server would mean patching that server forever.

2.5 Security and keeping it running

AWS Secrets ManagerSecurity

An encrypted vault for passwords and API keys.

Holds the Upstox Analytics Token, the news provider key and the LLM key. Code reads them at runtime; they never appear in source control.

Why this: it records who read which secret and when, and can alert before the Upstox token's one-year expiry.

AWS IAMSecurity

Decides which part of the system may do what.

Each Lambda gets its own role with the narrowest possible permission — the news fetcher cannot read the forecast database.

Why it matters: if one component is ever compromised, the damage stops at that component's permissions.

Amazon CloudWatchMonitoring

Collects logs, measurements and alarms.

Alerts when the live feed goes quiet, a day's candles are missing, forecast accuracy drops, or spend exceeds budget.

Why it matters: a data pipeline that fails silently produces wrong forecasts that look perfectly normal. Alarms are how you find out on day one, not day thirty.

AWS CDKDeployment

Describes the whole cloud setup as code.

Every service above is defined in TypeScript, in Git. Deploying is one command.

Why it matters: the system can be rebuilt from scratch in a fresh AWS account in under an hour, and every change is reviewable.

Services deliberately NOT used

Leaving things out is also a design decision. No NAT Gateway (~$35/mo) — the Upstox Analytics Token needs no static IP for market data, so no private networking is required. No ElastiCache — Aurora answers in milliseconds for a handful of users. No SageMaker real-time endpoints — forecasting runs once a day, so a 24/7 endpoint would be idle 99.8% of the time. SageMaker Training and Batch Transform are used; only the always-on endpoint is avoided. No Kinesis — the live feed goes straight from Fargate to the dashboard; there is no need to store every tick. Together these omissions save roughly $200/month.

3A day in the life of the system

What actually happens, hour by hour, on a normal trading day.

  1. 07:30 — Wake up and check the instrument list Downloads the daily Upstox instrument file so every stock's identifier is current. Identifiers change; hard-coding them silently fetches the wrong stock's data. EventBridge → Lambda → S3
  2. 08:00 — Collect news and macro data Pulls overnight business headlines and the latest oil, gold and USD/INR values. An LLM converts each article into structured numbers: sentiment, event type, companies affected. The original publication time is kept so the model can never see news from the future. Lambda → SQS → Lambda + LLM → S3
  3. 08:10 — Build the features Glue adjusts for any corporate action, computes the technical indicators, joins yesterday's news and macro features, and runs 14 data-quality checks. If a check fails, the pipeline stops here rather than forecasting on bad data. AWS Glue → S3
  4. 08:30 — Make the forecast The four models each answer their question for every stock. The Risk Engine checks the answers are coherent — low must be below high, close must sit between them — and assigns a risk score or returns No Trade. Fargate → Lambda → Aurora
  5. 09:15 — Market opens, live prices start A Fargate container connects to the Upstox WebSocket and decodes the binary feed. Prices stream to the dashboard. Individual ticks are not written to the database — that would be millions of pointless writes per day. ECS Fargate → WebSocket → Dashboard
  6. 15:30 — Market closes The streaming container shuts itself down. Nothing runs, nothing is billed. EventBridge stops the task
  7. 16:00 — Record what actually happened Downloads the day's official candles and compares them against forecasts whose horizon has now matured. This is the scoreboard: was the estimate right? Lambda → S3 → Aurora
  8. Sunday 02:00 — Retrain All four models retrain on data including the past week, are tested against simple baselines, and are only promoted if they beat them. If they do not, last week's models stay. Fargate → S3 → model registry

4The decisions worth explaining

4.1 Why Upstox and not Zerodha

Both are reputable Indian brokers with developer APIs. For this system — which only reads data and never places an order — Upstox is clearly better. The detail is in Document 3; the summary:

Upstox Analytics Token Chosen
  • Free — ₹0 per month, confirmed in the official documentation
  • Valid one year — generated once, not daily
  • No static IP needed for market data, history or WebSocket
  • Daily candles back to January 2000; 10 years per request
  • Read-only by design — it cannot place an order even by mistake
Zerodha Kite Connect Not chosen
  • ₹500/month — market data is not in the free tier
  • Token expires daily and renewal needs an interactive login with 2FA — it cannot be fully automated
  • Static IP required for orders, forcing a NAT Gateway (~$35/mo)
  • Trading-capable credentials in a system that should never trade
The decisive point

The Zerodha token must be regenerated every morning through a browser login with two-factor authentication. That is a person doing a manual step before 08:30, every single working day, forever. The Upstox token lasts a year. For a system that runs unattended, this difference matters more than the ₹500.

4.2 Why a database as well as S3

Putting forecasts only in S3 would mean scanning files to answer a dashboard query. Putting training data in Aurora would cost many times more and be slower for bulk reads.

4.3 How SageMaker is used — and how it is not

SageMaker is AWS's managed machine-learning platform. This system uses two parts of it and deliberately avoids a third.

PartUsed?What it does hereCost
Training JobYesFits the four models weekly, then shuts down~$1/mo
Batch TransformYesScores 50 rows daily in ~3 min, then shuts down~$2/mo
Real-time endpointNoA server held ready 24/7 for instant answers~$145/mo

The distinction matters. A real-time endpoint is what most SageMaker tutorials show, and it is the wrong shape for this system: the forecast runs once a day, so an always-on endpoint would sit idle 99.8% of the time and still bill for it. Batch Transform does the identical scoring work, bills only the three minutes it runs, and costs about a thirteenth as much.

Why SageMaker rather than a plain container: the training itself is small enough to run anywhere — XGBoost on 50 stocks × 5 years fits in minutes on an ordinary CPU, with no GPU needed. What SageMaker adds is the surrounding discipline: every training run is versioned, every model file is traceable to the exact data and code that produced it, and promotion from "trained" to "live" is an explicit, recorded step. For a forecasting system that must be auditable, that bookkeeping is the point — and it arrives without writing any of it ourselves.

What would change the answer: if inference were ever needed on demand rather than once a morning, the real-time endpoint becomes correct despite the cost. Nothing in the current design points that way.

4.4 Why four separate models

Each question has a different shape, so each gets its own model. Section 2 of Document 2 covers this in full, but briefly: the highest price over five days is a maximum of five numbers, which behaves differently from an average; and a probability must be calibrated, which is a different training objective entirely. One model forced to answer all four questions answers all of them worse.

The two data problems that break forecasting systems

1. Corporate actions. When a stock splits 1-for-5, the price drops 80% overnight. To a naive pipeline that looks like an 80% crash and poisons every calculation that touches that date — for a 200-day average, that is 200 subsequent days. Prices must be split-adjusted, and any single-day move above 25% is automatically flagged for review.

2. Point-in-time correctness. A model must only ever see what was genuinely knowable on that date. News carries its publication timestamp, not its ingestion timestamp. This sounds obvious and is violated constantly — it is the main reason backtests look brilliant and live performance does not.

Why baselines come before models (stage 3)

A baseline is a deliberately stupid prediction — "tomorrow's price equals today's". If a sophisticated model cannot beat it, the model is worthless no matter how impressive its accuracy number looks. Building baselines first means there is always something honest to compare against, and it is the single cheapest protection against fooling yourself.

How the Prediction Works

What the system forecasts, how it learns, how we test whether it learned anything real, and why the honest answer to "how accurate is it?" is a much smaller number than you might expect.

Version 3.0 · Date 29 October 2026 · Models 4 × XGBoost + risk engine · Horizons 1 and 5 trading days
Who this is for

Anyone who needs to understand or approve this system. No machine-learning background assumed. Technical specifics are in boxes you can skip without losing the thread.

1What the system actually predicts

Four separate questions about a stock over the next N trading days, plus a fifth step that decides whether to trust the answers at all.

#QuestionOutputHonest difficulty
1 Where will the price close? An estimated closing price in rupees, with a range Very hard
2 What is the highest it will reach? Estimated maximum price in the window Very hard
3 What is the lowest it will fall to? Estimated minimum price in the window Very hard
4 What is the chance it ends higher than today? A calibrated probability, e.g. 58% Hard, but measurable
5 Should we trust these four answers? A risk score, or No Trade Tractable
In plain words

Imagine asking four analysts about one stock. The first guesses the closing price. The second guesses the best it will touch. The third guesses the worst. The fourth does not guess a price at all — they only say how confident they are that it finishes up. Then a supervisor checks whether the four stories are consistent and whether anyone sounds like they are inventing it. That supervisor is question 5, and it is the most practically useful part of the system.

What "estimated maximum" does not mean

It is not a ceiling, a guarantee, or a promise the price will not exceed it. It is the model's best estimate of a typical high, and it will be wrong regularly — by design, roughly as often as it is right in either direction. Every number shown on screen carries its horizon, its uncertainty range, the model version and the time it was generated. A forecast without those four things is a rumour.

2Why four models instead of one

A reasonable question: why not one model that outputs everything? Because the four questions are mathematically different shapes, and a single model forced to answer all four answers each one worse.

QuestionWhat it really isRight tool
Closing pricePredicting one number near the middle of a distributionRegression on returns
Highest pricePredicting the maximum of five numbers — extreme-value behaviour, systematically skewed upwardSeparate regression with its own target
Lowest priceThe minimum of five numbers — skewed the other waySeparate regression
Gain probabilityNot a price at all. A probability that must be calibrated: when it says 60%, it must be right 60% of the timeClassification with calibration
Why the high/low distinction matters

Over five days, the average price and the highest price behave very differently. The average tends to sit near today's price. The maximum is almost always above it — simply because taking the largest of five random numbers pulls upward. A model trained to predict the average will consistently guess the maximum too low. Giving the maximum its own model and its own training target fixes this.

Keeping the four answers coherent

Four independent models can contradict each other — predicting a low of ₹2,400, a high of ₹2,380 and a close of ₹2,500 is nonsense. A consistency check runs on every forecast:

predicted_low  ≤  predicted_close  ≤  predicted_high
predicted_low  ≤  current_price    ≤  predicted_high   (usually; flagged if not)
predicted_high − predicted_low     ≥  typical daily range

A forecast failing these checks is not shown. It is logged, flagged, and the stock returns No Trade for that day. Contradiction is a signal that the models are uncertain, and that is information worth surfacing rather than hiding.

5How the models learn

5.1 What XGBoost is

In plain words

XGBoost builds hundreds of small decision trees, one after another. The first tree makes a crude guess. The second tree studies where the first was wrong and corrects it. The third corrects what remains. After a few hundred rounds, the combined result is far better than any single tree.

It is the standard choice for table-shaped data like this, it trains in minutes on an ordinary CPU, and it can report which features mattered most — which matters when a human has to explain a forecast to someone else.

5.2 The training target is the change, never the price

The single most important technical decision in this document

The models are trained to predict the percentage change, not the price. The rupee figure shown on screen is computed afterwards from today's price.

Why this matters so much: if you train a model to predict tomorrow's price, it quickly discovers that "tomorrow ≈ today" is almost right every single day. It will score R² above 0.99 and look extraordinary — while having learned absolutely nothing about what will change. This one mistake is responsible for the great majority of published stock-prediction results claiming 95%+ accuracy.

Predicting the change removes that illusion. The scores look much worse, and they are honest.

5.3 Walk-forward testing

Ordinary machine learning shuffles data randomly into training and testing piles. For time series that is catastrophic — it lets the model learn from June to predict March.

Instead, testing walks forward through time, the way reality does:

Fold 1   train 2021─2022  │ gap │ test 2023 Q1
Fold 2   train 2021─2023Q1│ gap │ test 2023 Q2
Fold 3   train 2021─2023Q2│ gap │ test 2023 Q3
Fold 4   train 2021─2023Q3│ gap │ test 2023 Q4
                            ▲
                   5-day gap, always
Why the gap exists

Because the forecast looks 5 days ahead, the last day of training has an answer that only becomes known 5 days into the test period. Without a gap, a sliver of the test period leaks backwards into training. The model would effectively see a few days of its own exam paper.

The gap is small, easily forgotten, and removing it inflates results noticeably. It is one of the most common silent errors in financial backtesting.

5.4 Making the probability honest

A raw model probability of 0.7 does not mean a 70% chance. Models are routinely overconfident. Calibration fixes this by comparing predicted probabilities against what actually happened on held-out data and correcting the mapping.

Model saysReality (uncalibrated)After calibration
90% chance of gainActually right 62% of the timeReported as 62%
70% chance of gainActually right 56% of the timeReported as 56%
55% chance of gainActually right 53% of the timeReported as 53%

An uncalibrated probability is just a ranking number wearing a percentage sign. If calibration error exceeds the threshold, the model is blocked from promotion — it does not reach the dashboard.

6The baselines — the honesty test

Before any model is trusted, it must beat deliberately stupid alternatives. This is the most important section in this document.

Why B2 deserves special attention

"Always predict up" sounds absurd. But Indian equity markets rose in most years of any 5-year window, so this baseline can score 55% or higher on directional accuracy without containing a single idea.

This is exactly how impressive-sounding accuracy claims are manufactured — usually without anyone intending to deceive. A model reporting "58% accurate" sounds good until you learn the always-up baseline scored 56% on the same data. The real contribution was two points, and it may not survive transaction costs.

Every accuracy figure this system reports is shown next to its baseline. A number without its baseline is not a result.

The rule that protects this project

In stock forecasting, results that look too good are far more likely to be bugs than breakthroughs. The known causes are all mundane: predicting price instead of change, leaking future information through a scaler or a timestamp, or forgetting the gap between training and testing.

So a metric that is too high blocks promotion exactly as a failing metric does, until someone explains why. This is automated in the promotion gate, not left to discipline.

Why R² near zero is the correct outcome

R² measures how much of the variation a model explains. For 5-day stock returns on liquid large-caps, honest R² is near zero. That is not failure — it is what efficient markets look like. If large, easy predictability existed in NIFTY 50 stocks, well-capitalised firms with faster systems would have removed it already.

The useful question is not "can we predict the market?" but "is there a small, measurable, repeatable edge, and does it survive costs?" A small honest edge, properly measured, is worth far more than a large imaginary one.

8Guarding against fooling ourselves

Data leakage is when the model accidentally sees the future. It is the number one cause of models that work beautifully in testing and fail completely in practice.

The shuffled-label test — the strongest check available

Take the training data and randomise every answer, so there is genuinely nothing to learn. Retrain. The model must now perform no better than random.

If it still shows skill, information is reaching it through a path that is not the intended features — and no amount of code review finds that as reliably as this one test. It runs automatically before any model is promoted.

Why No Trade is a feature, not a shortfall

A forecasting system that always produces an answer is less useful than one that admits uncertainty. Markets are frequently unpredictable, and a system that says "today I do not know" on those days is behaving correctly.

Expect No Trade on a substantial fraction of stock-days. That is the engine working, not the engine failing. A system that confidently forecasts all 50 stocks every single day would be the worrying one.

The cost check deserves emphasis

Round-trip costs on Indian equity delivery trades — brokerage, STT, exchange charges, stamp duty, GST and realistic slippage — come to roughly 0.25–0.30%. If the model predicts a 0.2% move, there is no opportunity even if the prediction is perfectly correct. The engine filters these out before they reach the screen.

10Keeping models honest over time

10.1 Weekly retraining, not daily

Models retrain every Sunday on data including the past week. Not daily, because one day adds about 0.1% new information to the training set while requiring a full re-validation to be trustworthy — the cost is real and the benefit is not.

10.2 The promotion gate

A newly trained model replaces the current one only if it passes every check:

  1. Beats the baselinesIncluding the always-up baseline, by a margin wide enough to not be chance.
  2. Probabilities are calibratedCalibration error below threshold, with a reliability chart produced for review.
  3. Forecasts are coherentLow ≤ close ≤ high holds across the test period.
  4. Leakage tests passIncluding the shuffled-label test.
  5. Nothing is suspiciously goodNo metric above its red-flag threshold without an explanation.
  6. Not materially worse than currentPrevents a bad week quietly degrading the system.

If a model fails, the previous model stays in service and an alert is raised. If no model has ever passed, the system shows no forecasts at all rather than unvalidated ones.

Instrument identifiers change — on symbol changes, mergers and corporate restructuring. A hard-coded key that silently starts resolving to a different instrument produces data that is perfectly well-formed and completely wrong. Nothing errors. The model simply learns from the wrong company's prices.

The daily refresh compares every tracked symbol's key against yesterday's and raises an alarm on any change. This is a 20-line safeguard that prevents a class of failure that is otherwise nearly invisible.

For a private, internal research MVP — invite-only dashboard, no external users, no public display — the risk is low and the activity is ordinary. Build the system, prove the models work or do not, and resolve the rights question in parallel.

What it does block is any public launch. The clarification should be requested in Week 1 so the answer arrives long before it is needed, rather than becoming an emergency later.

Roughly two days of work. Do these before the full build, not during it.

  1. Generate the token and make one call Confirms the account is active with eligible segments. Upstox can reject API access when an account has no active segments, so this is worth proving on day one. ~1 hour

  5. Deliberately exceed the rate limit Confirm the error response looks as expected and that backoff handling works before it happens during a real backfill. ~1 hour
  6. Send the data-rights questions The three questions in Section 6. Start the clock early — a written answer may take weeks. ~1 hour

How the prediction is calculated

The part everyone asks about. It is more ordinary than it sounds.

What "training" actually means

There is no stock-prediction service to call. No API returns tomorrow's price, and a general AI like ChatGPT or Claude cannot do this — it has no access to market data and was never built for it. The model has to be trained on our own data.

XGBoost arrives as an empty algorithm. It knows how to find patterns in tables; it knows nothing about stocks, RSI or RELIANCE. All of its knowledge comes from the 50,000 rows we show it.

How XGBoost finds those patterns

It builds hundreds of small decision trees, where each new tree corrects the mistakes of the ones before it.

Boosting — why one weak tree becomes a strong model
True answer: +2.00%

  Tree 1 guesses +0.50%   -> still 1.50% short
  Tree 2 studies only that 1.50% leftover, guesses +0.90%   -> 0.60% short
  Tree 3 studies only that 0.60% leftover, guesses +0.40%   -> 0.20% short
  Tree 4 studies only that 0.20% leftover, guesses +0.10%   -> 0.10% short
  ...
  Tree 300

  Final = 0.50 + 0.90 + 0.40 + 0.10 + ... = +1.95%

One tree alone is poor. Three hundred, each fixing the last one's error, is strong. We use XGBoost rather than a neural network because it is consistently better on table-shaped data, trains in minutes on an ordinary CPU, and can report which features mattered — which matters when a person has to explain a forecast.

The mistake that produces fake 99% accuracy

If you train a model to predict the price, it scores beautifully — R² above 0.99 — and is completely useless. It has learned "RELIANCE is around ₹2,400", which is true and tells you nothing about tomorrow.

Train on the change, never the level. Every one of the 40 features and all four answers in this system are ratios or percentages for exactly this reason.

Say this out loud to any audience

Realistic accuracy for short-horizon direction on liquid large-cap equities is roughly 52–56%. Published claims of 95%+ almost always come from measuring the wrong thing — usually predicting the price level rather than the change, which scores beautifully while predicting nothing useful.

If this system ever reports above 60% on unseen data, the correct first response is to look for a bug. In this field, results that are too good are far more often mistakes than discoveries.

A legitimate outcome

It is entirely possible that the models show no reliable edge. Markets are close to efficient and that result is expected often enough to plan for.

Measuring honestly and reporting "no edge found" is a successful outcome for this project — considerably better than shipping a model that only looked good because the test was wrong. The AWS architecture, the pipeline and the discipline are what the demo is actually showing.

What this system does not do

It does not place orders, connect to a broker for trading, hold money, or give investment advice. There is no execution path. The system ends at step 10 with a person reading a screen and deciding for themselves.

1Two pipelines, one screen

The single idea that resolves most of the confusion. Both end at the dashboard. They share nothing else.

Live prices — NOT stored

Where from — Upstox WebSocket, open 09:15–15:30.

How often — every second, roughly 20,000 ticks per stock per day.

Where it goes — straight from Fargate to the browser.

What is kept — only the latest price per stock, in memory. 50 numbers. The next tick overwrites it.

Used for prediction? — No. Never.

Daily candles — stored

Where from — Upstox REST API, one call after the close.

How often — once a day, 50 rows.

Where it goes — S3, permanently.

What is kept — open, high, low, close, volume. Five numbers per stock per day, back five years.

Used for prediction? — Yes. This is the only data the model sees.

2Why live prices are not stored

Four reasons, any one of which would be sufficient.

#ReasonThe detail
1The volume is enormous50 stocks × ~20,000 ticks = 1,000,000 rows per day, or 250 million a year. Real storage cost, real query cost.
2The model cannot use themIt was trained on completed daily candles. A tick from 11:47 is data in a shape it has never seen; the output would be meaningless.
3Upstox already stores itTheir history API goes back to the year 2000 and is free. Why pay to duplicate what someone else keeps for nothing?
4Nobody would read itNo one will ever ask what RELIANCE traded at 11:47:23 last March. Stored, it costs money forever and is read by nobody.
What the live container actually holds

A Fargate container keeps a dictionary of 50 entries — one current price per stock. A new tick overwrites the old value. When the market closes the container stops and that memory is gone.

Why Fargate and not Lambda: the trading session is 6 hours 15 minutes. Lambda stops at 15 minutes. It is not a preference; Lambda physically cannot hold that connection.

3What we do store

Three things, in two places, for two different reasons.

WhatWhereSizeWhy it lives there
Daily candles, 5 years × 50 stocksS3~200 MBBulk history, read all at once during training. S3 is cheapest for this.
40 features per stock per dayS3~500 MBExpensive to recompute; stored so training and forecasting just read it.
Forecasts — today's and every past oneAuroraa few MBThe dashboard needs one stock's row in milliseconds. That is a database job.
Live ticksnowhere—See section 2.

Why two stores and not one

Amazon S3 — the warehouse

Cheap, enormous, built for reading large amounts sequentially.

Good at: give me 5 years of RELIANCE

Bad at: give me RELIANCE right now — it would scan files to answer.

~$3/month.

Aurora PostgreSQL — the front desk

Fast for single lookups, small, more expensive per gigabyte.

Good at: give me RELIANCE right now — milliseconds.

Bad at: holding bulk history — costs many times S3 for the same data.

~$45/month.

Using one for the other's job is the classic mistake. Forecasts in S3 means scanning files to render a web page. Training history in Aurora means paying database prices for cold storage.

5Does the forecast change during the day?

No. It is calculated once at 08:30 and stays identical until tomorrow morning.

The blue line is written once at 08:30. The red line is today's reality.

#Why it does not moveExplanation
1The inputs have not changedIt was built from yesterday's completed candle plus 200+ days of history. Today's candle does not exist until 15:30.
2The model cannot accept intraday dataTrained on completed daily candles. An 11:00 price is a shape it has never seen.
3It is a 5-day forecastA prediction at 09:20 and one at 09:21 cover nearly the same window. Recalculating would return the same number.
The useful thing you can still show live

The forecast is frozen, but the gap between the live price and the forecast moves all day — and that is genuinely informative:

11:00 — live ₹236, target ₹248 → +5.1% to target
14:00 — live ₹243, target ₹248 → +2.1% to target

It is calculated in the browser from two numbers you already have. Costs nothing, and it makes the dashboard feel alive without touching the model.

Put the timestamp on the card

Show “calculated 08:30” next to the forecast. Without it, the first question from any viewer is whether the number is stale or broken. With it, the design reads as deliberate.

6What happens tomorrow

A brand new forecast. The old one is kept, but it is no longer what you display.

Why a fresh forecast rather than reusing yesterday's

By Tuesday morning you know one more trading day — Monday's actual close, its volume, what NIFTY did. The RSI has changed, the moving averages have moved, the volatility has updated.

All 40 features are different. Reusing yesterday's number would mean ignoring a full day of real information.

Overwrite the display, never the record

The dashboard shows the newest forecast per stock. But every forecast ever made stays in the table, because that history is the only way to prove the system works.

No stored predictions means no measured accuracy. No measured accuracy means no demo — just a number on a screen that nobody ever checked.
