How the Prediction Works

What the system forecasts, how it learns, how we test whether it learned anything real, and why the honest answer to "how accurate is it?" is a much smaller number than you might expect.

Version 3.0 · Date 29 October 2026 · Models 4 × XGBoost + risk engine · Horizons 1 and 5 trading days
Who this is for

Anyone who needs to understand or approve this system. No machine-learning background assumed. Technical specifics are in boxes you can skip without losing the thread.

1What the system actually predicts

Four separate questions about a stock over the next N trading days, plus a fifth step that decides whether to trust the answers at all.

#QuestionOutputHonest difficulty
1 Where will the price close? An estimated closing price in rupees, with a range Very hard
2 What is the highest it will reach? Estimated maximum price in the window Very hard
3 What is the lowest it will fall to? Estimated minimum price in the window Very hard
4 What is the chance it ends higher than today? A calibrated probability, e.g. 58% Hard, but measurable
5 Should we trust these four answers? A risk score, or No Trade Tractable
In plain words

Imagine asking four analysts about one stock. The first guesses the closing price. The second guesses the best it will touch. The third guesses the worst. The fourth does not guess a price at all — they only say how confident they are that it finishes up. Then a supervisor checks whether the four stories are consistent and whether anyone sounds like they are inventing it. That supervisor is question 5, and it is the most practically useful part of the system.

What "estimated maximum" does not mean

It is not a ceiling, a guarantee, or a promise the price will not exceed it. It is the model's best estimate of a typical high, and it will be wrong regularly — by design, roughly as often as it is right in either direction. Every number shown on screen carries its horizon, its uncertainty range, the model version and the time it was generated. A forecast without those four things is a rumour.

2Why four models instead of one

A reasonable question: why not one model that outputs everything? Because the four questions are mathematically different shapes, and a single model forced to answer all four answers each one worse.

QuestionWhat it really isRight tool
Closing pricePredicting one number near the middle of a distributionRegression on returns
Highest pricePredicting the maximum of five numbers — extreme-value behaviour, systematically skewed upwardSeparate regression with its own target
Lowest priceThe minimum of five numbers — skewed the other waySeparate regression
Gain probabilityNot a price at all. A probability that must be calibrated: when it says 60%, it must be right 60% of the timeClassification with calibration
Why the high/low distinction matters

Over five days, the average price and the highest price behave very differently. The average tends to sit near today's price. The maximum is almost always above it — simply because taking the largest of five random numbers pulls upward. A model trained to predict the average will consistently guess the maximum too low. Giving the maximum its own model and its own training target fixes this.

Keeping the four answers coherent

Four independent models can contradict each other — predicting a low of ₹2,400, a high of ₹2,380 and a close of ₹2,500 is nonsense. A consistency check runs on every forecast:

predicted_low  ≤  predicted_close  ≤  predicted_high
predicted_low  ≤  current_price    ≤  predicted_high   (usually; flagged if not)
predicted_high − predicted_low     ≥  typical daily range

A forecast failing these checks is not shown. It is logged, flagged, and the stock returns No Trade for that day. Contradiction is a signal that the models are uncertain, and that is information worth surfacing rather than hiding.

News must prove itself

News features are not assumed to help. The plan is to build a price-only model first, measure it, then add news and measure again. If the news version is not meaningfully better, news is dropped — saving the provider subscription and the LLM cost.

This matters because news is the most leak-prone data in the system. An article dated "15 March" may have been published at 11pm, after the market closed. Training on it as if it were available at 9am that morning gives the model tomorrow's newspaper, and produces spectacular backtest results that evaporate in live use. Every article is keyed to its publication timestamp, never its date.

5How the models learn

5.1 What XGBoost is

In plain words

XGBoost builds hundreds of small decision trees, one after another. The first tree makes a crude guess. The second tree studies where the first was wrong and corrects it. The third corrects what remains. After a few hundred rounds, the combined result is far better than any single tree.

It is the standard choice for table-shaped data like this, it trains in minutes on an ordinary CPU, and it can report which features mattered most — which matters when a human has to explain a forecast to someone else.

5.2 The training target is the change, never the price

The single most important technical decision in this document

The models are trained to predict the percentage change, not the price. The rupee figure shown on screen is computed afterwards from today's price.

Why this matters so much: if you train a model to predict tomorrow's price, it quickly discovers that "tomorrow ≈ today" is almost right every single day. It will score R² above 0.99 and look extraordinary — while having learned absolutely nothing about what will change. This one mistake is responsible for the great majority of published stock-prediction results claiming 95%+ accuracy.

Predicting the change removes that illusion. The scores look much worse, and they are honest.

5.3 Walk-forward testing

Ordinary machine learning shuffles data randomly into training and testing piles. For time series that is catastrophic — it lets the model learn from June to predict March.

Instead, testing walks forward through time, the way reality does:

Fold 1   train 2021─2022  │ gap │ test 2023 Q1
Fold 2   train 2021─2023Q1│ gap │ test 2023 Q2
Fold 3   train 2021─2023Q2│ gap │ test 2023 Q3
Fold 4   train 2021─2023Q3│ gap │ test 2023 Q4
                            ▲
                   5-day gap, always
Why the gap exists

Because the forecast looks 5 days ahead, the last day of training has an answer that only becomes known 5 days into the test period. Without a gap, a sliver of the test period leaks backwards into training. The model would effectively see a few days of its own exam paper.

The gap is small, easily forgotten, and removing it inflates results noticeably. It is one of the most common silent errors in financial backtesting.

5.4 Making the probability honest

A raw model probability of 0.7 does not mean a 70% chance. Models are routinely overconfident. Calibration fixes this by comparing predicted probabilities against what actually happened on held-out data and correcting the mapping.

Model saysReality (uncalibrated)After calibration
90% chance of gainActually right 62% of the timeReported as 62%
70% chance of gainActually right 56% of the timeReported as 56%
55% chance of gainActually right 53% of the timeReported as 53%

An uncalibrated probability is just a ranking number wearing a percentage sign. If calibration error exceeds the threshold, the model is blocked from promotion — it does not reach the dashboard.

6The baselines — the honesty test

Before any model is trusted, it must beat deliberately stupid alternatives. This is the most important section in this document.

BaselineWhat it doesWhy it is included
B1 · No changePredicts tomorrow equals todayShockingly hard to beat on price
B2 · Always upAlways predicts a gainIndian markets rose most years. This alone can score 55%+
B3 · Historical averageUses this stock's long-run average returnTests whether any drift is exploitable
B4 · Yesterday's rangeUses recent high/low as the forecastBaseline for the high and low models
B5 · MomentumContinues the recent trendA simple, well-documented market factor
B6 · Price-only modelThe full model without news or macroMeasures whether news earns its cost
Why B2 deserves special attention

"Always predict up" sounds absurd. But Indian equity markets rose in most years of any 5-year window, so this baseline can score 55% or higher on directional accuracy without containing a single idea.

This is exactly how impressive-sounding accuracy claims are manufactured — usually without anyone intending to deceive. A model reporting "58% accurate" sounds good until you learn the always-up baseline scored 56% on the same data. The real contribution was two points, and it may not survive transaction costs.

Every accuracy figure this system reports is shown next to its baseline. A number without its baseline is not a result.

The rule that protects this project

In stock forecasting, results that look too good are far more likely to be bugs than breakthroughs. The known causes are all mundane: predicting price instead of change, leaking future information through a scaler or a timestamp, or forgetting the gap between training and testing.

So a metric that is too high blocks promotion exactly as a failing metric does, until someone explains why. This is automated in the promotion gate, not left to discipline.

Why R² near zero is the correct outcome

R² measures how much of the variation a model explains. For 5-day stock returns on liquid large-caps, honest R² is near zero. That is not failure — it is what efficient markets look like. If large, easy predictability existed in NIFTY 50 stocks, well-capitalised firms with faster systems would have removed it already.

The useful question is not "can we predict the market?" but "is there a small, measurable, repeatable edge, and does it survive costs?" A small honest edge, properly measured, is worth far more than a large imaginary one.

8Guarding against fooling ourselves

Data leakage is when the model accidentally sees the future. It is the number one cause of models that work beautifully in testing and fail completely in practice.

LeakHow it sneaks inGuard
Future pricesRolling window accidentally includes the target dayEvery feature recomputed from truncated history and compared
Scaler leakageNormalising across all data before splitting — the scaler has seen future highs and lowsScalers fitted on the training fold only
Overlapping labels5-day targets overlap the test period boundaryMandatory 5-day gap between train and test
News timestampsUsing article date rather than publication timeMinute-level publication timestamps; evening news is next-day news
Survivorship biasTraining on today's NIFTY 50 list using old data — the list already reflects who did wellPoint-in-time index membership per date
Corporate actionsAn unadjusted 1:5 split looks like an 80% crashSplit adjustment plus automatic flag above 25% daily move
The shuffled-label test — the strongest check available

Take the training data and randomise every answer, so there is genuinely nothing to learn. Retrain. The model must now perform no better than random.

If it still shows skill, information is reaching it through a path that is not the intended features — and no amount of code review finds that as reliably as this one test. It runs automatically before any model is promoted.

9The Risk & Opportunity Engine

Not a fifth prediction model. A rule-based layer that decides whether the four forecasts are worth showing.

CheckQuestion askedIf it fails
CoherenceDo low ≤ close ≤ high hold?No Trade — models disagree
UncertaintyIs the predicted range unusually wide?Risk score raised; may force No Trade
LiquidityDoes this stock trade enough to act on?Excluded
DownsideHow bad is the realistic worst case?Flagged prominently in the UI
Reward vs riskIs upside meaningfully larger than downside?Low opportunity score
Cost realityDoes the expected move exceed trading costs?No Trade — not worth acting on
Model healthIs the model still beating its baseline recently?All forecasts suspended
Data freshnessIs any input stale or missing?No Trade
Why No Trade is a feature, not a shortfall

A forecasting system that always produces an answer is less useful than one that admits uncertainty. Markets are frequently unpredictable, and a system that says "today I do not know" on those days is behaving correctly.

Expect No Trade on a substantial fraction of stock-days. That is the engine working, not the engine failing. A system that confidently forecasts all 50 stocks every single day would be the worrying one.

The cost check deserves emphasis

Round-trip costs on Indian equity delivery trades — brokerage, STT, exchange charges, stamp duty, GST and realistic slippage — come to roughly 0.25–0.30%. If the model predicts a 0.2% move, there is no opportunity even if the prediction is perfectly correct. The engine filters these out before they reach the screen.

10Keeping models honest over time

10.1 Weekly retraining, not daily

Models retrain every Sunday on data including the past week. Not daily, because one day adds about 0.1% new information to the training set while requiring a full re-validation to be trustworthy — the cost is real and the benefit is not.

10.2 The promotion gate

A newly trained model replaces the current one only if it passes every check:

  1. Beats the baselinesIncluding the always-up baseline, by a margin wide enough to not be chance.
  2. Probabilities are calibratedCalibration error below threshold, with a reliability chart produced for review.
  3. Forecasts are coherentLow ≤ close ≤ high holds across the test period.
  4. Leakage tests passIncluding the shuffled-label test.
  5. Nothing is suspiciously goodNo metric above its red-flag threshold without an explanation.
  6. Not materially worse than currentPrevents a bad week quietly degrading the system.

If a model fails, the previous model stays in service and an alert is raised. If no model has ever passed, the system shows no forecasts at all rather than unvalidated ones.

10.3 Watching for decay

MonitorWatches forAction
Rolling accuracy60-day accuracy falling toward baselineAlert; investigate
Edge decayAdvantage over baseline reaching zeroConsider suspending forecasts
Calibration driftProbabilities becoming unreliableRefit calibration
Feature driftInputs looking unlike training dataAlert; possible regime change
Volatility regimeMarket far calmer or wilder than training periodWiden ranges; flag forecasts

The edge-decay monitor is the one that matters most. A model whose advantage has gone to zero is no longer providing value, and the honest response is to stop showing its forecasts — not to keep displaying them because the system is already built.

Why the baseline appears on screen

Showing "54.1% accurate" alone invites the reader to think the system is good. Showing it beside "baseline 52.0%, edge +2.1 points, range +0.3 to +3.9" tells the truth: there is a small measured advantage, and the range shows how certain we are about it.

Carrying that context onto the screen — rather than burying it in a report nobody opens — is a deliberate design decision. It makes it difficult for anyone to present a forecast without its honest framing.

12What this system cannot do

CannotWhy
Predict news before it happensNothing in the data contains tomorrow's announcements. A model can react to news, never anticipate it.
Handle unprecedented eventsA model learns from history. A pandemic, war or policy shock has no precedent in the training window.
Guarantee the predicted high or lowThey are estimates of typical outcomes. Prices exceed them regularly.
Beat the market reliablyNo evidence it can, and this document does not claim it. The goal is measurement, not profit.
Work unchanged foreverMarkets shift. Hence weekly retraining and decay monitoring.
Replace judgementIt is a decision-support tool. A person reads it, with its uncertainty, and decides.
The sentence to repeat to any audience

This system produces measured, honest, uncertainty-aware forecasts with a small statistical edge — if an edge exists at all. It does not produce reliable profit, and it is not investment advice. Its real value is that every number it shows can be traced, reproduced and checked.
